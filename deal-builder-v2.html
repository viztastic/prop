How the stake is priced

The stake is priced against the whole journey to market ready, rather than against the phase you are committing to today. That is deliberate, and it is the part most worth understanding before anything is signed.

If you stop at a phase boundary you owe nothing further. The stake simply reflects the part of the journey that is not yet funded, and equity is earned as that work is actually delivered against the agreed requirements. Nothing on this page commits either side to a phase beyond the one being signed.

So the stake comes down as the journey gets funded, in cash or in venues, whichever you prefer. Fund all of it and we sit at the floor. Fund the minimum and we sit near the ceiling, which is where the risk sits too.

You are committing to run rialto at $500,000, but market ready costs $1,300,000. So while $460,000 is deferred on this phase, $1,260,000 of the road to a platform worth owning is still unfunded. That gap is why the stake sits at 67.8% rather than something smaller. Fund more of the journey and it comes down.

Cheaper money to try before this

The stake below is real and available, and it is also the most expensive capital here. Every route below is cheaper, and each one that lands moves every number on this page in your favour.

  • Venture capital. Venues two through fifty make this a software business, not a gym expense.
  • Private equity and family offices. Wellness roll-ups are buying in Melbourne, and some want the venue network more than the software.
  • Debt and asset finance. Lenders will lend against contracted recurring revenue.
  • R&D tax incentive. Roughly 43.5% refundable on eligible spend.
  • Grants. State and federal digital adoption and commercialisation programs.
  • SAFEs, convertible notes and revenue-linked notes. Defer the valuation argument, or price the risk without touching the cap table.
  • Pre-sales. Founding-venue pricing paid annually in advance, which is capital and proof of demand at the same time.
  • Employer prepayments. Corporates paying twelve months up front fund the wedge that serves them.
  • Strategic partners. Stripe, Xero, hardware vendors and insurers all run partner programs.

Try these first, and run them in parallel with discovery. Three weeks is roughly how long a first round of those conversations takes, so the phase 2 decision arrives with both answers in hand.

What the structure is

  • A new company holds SFIDI OS. Not your gym. Rialto, its members and its revenue stay entirely yours and are never part of this.
  • You contribute what we cannot buy: the flagship venue, the operating expertise, the first customer, the brand and the operator network. We contribute the build.
  • The stake is priced against the journey to market ready. Clear it in cash or by bringing paying venues. Both count identically, and the stake ratchets from 70% toward a 40% floor.
  • The floor holds at 40%. That is where a technical co-founder who funded and built the product lands, and the ratchet stops there.
  • One instrument. The equity is the entire price of the deferral, with no interest or premium on top.
  • Protections both ways: information rights, tag-along and drag-along, pre-emptive rights, and a shareholders agreement your adviser marks up.

Co-branding and reference rights, agreed upfront

These attach the moment any equity is issued, so there is nothing to negotiate later. They cost you no cash and they are a large part of why the stake is priced the way it is rather than higher.

  • Founding venue billing. SFIDI named and shown as the founding venue on sfidi-os.com and in every deck, in perpetuity.
  • Named client and logo rights, irrevocable, across our site, decks, proposals and marketing.
  • Co-branded case study, refreshed annually, with every figure and claim approved by you before publication.
  • Reference site. Rialto available for prospect visits, up to six a year, at times you choose.
  • Client referrals. Warm introductions across your Melbourne operator network, and joint presence at two industry events a year.
  • Venue photography and named quotes, with approval rights on each, held in perpetuity once approved.
  • It survives a change of control. If the business is later sold or recapitalised, these do not lapse.

Approval rights stay with you on every published claim, figure and image. The fact of the relationship is settled here, and the published detail stays yours to approve.
