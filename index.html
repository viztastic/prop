01 Where the work stands

What exists is a complete, high-fidelity specification. What does not exist is a product.

Done, and genuinely valuable

  • Seven high-fidelity prototypes, the whole product clickable across five personas, plus brand site, venue site and competitive read.
  • Final design language: colour, type, spacing, radii, shadows and copy resolved to build standard.
  • Architecture settled: twelve module boundaries, dependency direction, eight invariants, public-API-only.
  • Eight accepted ADRs. The expensive decisions are made and written down.
  • Entitlement resolution modelled as a pure function with a case table, which is the hardest rule in the system.

This de-risks the build and is a large part of why five people can credibly commit to the dates below.

Still to build, which is all of it

  • No production code. Roughly 830 lines exist across a theme, six components and one domain rule.
  • No database, no API, no money. No schema, event log, ledger, Stripe integration, billing run or dunning.
  • No member app, storefront or embed widget.
  • No reporting, migration tooling or access-hardware integration.
  • None of the five capabilities raised at the preview.

The prototypes are mockups. They are convincing on a screen and nothing behind them is real. Every row in section 04 is work still to be done.

08 How to pay for it

Cash is cheapest and leaves nothing behind. If timing rather than the number is the obstacle, there are more routes open than most people assume, and they are combinable. Everything here is open for discussion.

Read this before Option A

There is a long list of cheaper money to try first

Option A exists because it is genuinely available, not because it is where anyone should start. It is the most expensive capital on this page and we would rather you exhausted the cheap routes before giving away any of the platform. Most of these are open to you right now and none of them has been tested yet.

  • Venture capital. A platform aimed at venues two through fifty is a software business with a real market, not a gym expense. That is a seed story, and the wedge is a genuinely differentiated one.
  • Private equity and family offices. Wellness and fitness roll-ups are actively acquiring in Melbourne. Some of that money wants the venue network more than the software.
  • Debt and asset finance. Banks and non-bank lenders will lend against contracted recurring revenue. A gym with a stable member base is a more bankable borrower than most software companies.
  • The R&D tax incentive. Roughly 43.5% refundable on eligible spend, covered in detail below. On this build that is a six-figure number.
  • Grants. State and federal digital adoption and commercialisation programs exist and are routinely under-claimed by businesses that assume they will not qualify.
  • Convertible instruments. A SAFE or a convertible note defers the valuation argument entirely, and a revenue-linked note prices the risk without touching the cap table.
  • Pre-sales. Sell venues two through ten before they exist. Founding-venue pricing, paid annually in advance, is the cheapest capital in the world because it is also proof of demand.
  • Employer prepayments. Corporate clients paying twelve months in advance for subsidised memberships fund the wedge that serves them.
  • Strategic partners. Stripe, Xero, access-hardware vendors and insurers all run partner programs with money and distribution attached.
  • Vendor financing. Us, which is Option A. Last on the list on purpose.

The reason to say this plainly: a structure where we fund the build and hold most of the platform company should be the option you take after the cheaper ones have been tried and turned down, not instead of trying them. If one of the routes above lands, every number in section 08 moves in your favour and we would genuinely rather that happened. Discovery is three weeks, which is roughly how long a first round of those conversations takes anyway.

Separately and free: invoicing weighted away from November and December into the January to March intake surge. A seven-month build fits inside one intake cycle; a thirteen-month one does not.

The lever most people forget: the R&D Tax Incentive

An Australian company under $20M turnover can claim a refundable offset of roughly 43.5% on eligible R&D. Software qualifies where the outcome is genuinely uncertain and the work is systematic and experimental. On this build the entitlement resolution function, the pricing engine, cross-venue settlement, the token ledger and deferred revenue recognition all plausibly qualify.

It substantiates unusually well: ADRs record the hypothesis and the decision, dated; test-first produces a literal experimental record; the event log timestamps everything. At 40% eligible spend the offset is roughly $194,000 on the full engagement, or $69,000 on Run Rialto.

The interaction nobody mentions until it is too late

The offset follows expenditure actually incurred. Cash you pay, upfront or as repayment, is expenditure, so deferring moves the claim into a later year rather than losing it. But value settled by issuing shares is not cash you spent, and neither is a balance retired by bringing venues. On a maximum-deferral structure most of the build could fall into that category, so Option A at its most aggressive and a large R&D claim may not both be available. This is not tax advice and we are not your advisers. Speak to your R&D adviser before signing, because the contract structure affects the answer. We will produce the technical documentation to support a claim at no charge.

What we will not do

  • Take a share of the venue's trading revenue, or of the gym. Every equity structure here attaches to a platform company and nothing else.
  • Build it entirely for free. We have payroll. The $40,000 minimum is the floor on any structure.
  • Ratchet our stake to zero. 40% is the floor wherever we funded the build.
  • Charge deferral interest and take equity. One or the other, and here it is equity.
  • Fix-price transferable-value N5 before counsel has given a position. We would price the worst case and you would pay for it.
  • Cut the test discipline to lower the number or hit a date. It is roughly 20% of the cost and it is why a billing engine can be trusted.
  • Quietly de-scope to hit a budget. Every cut is explicit, priced and yours to choose.
  • Promise the calendar without your side of section 03. If that is not realistic, we quote the longer one.
